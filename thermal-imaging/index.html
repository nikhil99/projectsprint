How far is India from a 100% indigenous thermal sight?

Thermal imagers see heat, so they work in total darkness, smoke and fog. Every soldier's night sight, every tank sight, every surveillance drone and every counter-drone tracker needs one. India designs and builds many of its own: Tonbo Imaging, which listed on the stock market in October 2026, BEL and others supply the forces. But the infrared detector at the heart of each one, about a third of its cost, is imported. The government's own defence-innovation programme says these detectors are not made in India. This tracker scores each layer, names the companies and the money, and lists what still blocks a thermal sight that is Indian all the way down.

India's defence-innovation programme, iDEX, notes that infrared detectors are not manufactured in India and are imported, particularly from Europe, and has asked startups to build them.

Who owns that value

An Indian sight around a foreign eye

About 29% of the value of an Indian-made sight is Indian-owned. India writes the software, designs the electronics and builds the housing, but the detector, the germanium in the lens and the eyepiece display are imported.

Why the detector is the chokepoint

A microbolometer is a grid of hundreds of thousands of tiny heat-sensitive bridges built on a chip and sealed in vacuum. It needs a MEMS fab, a readout chip and vacuum packaging. A handful of companies in France, Israel, the US and China make them, and exports are licensed.

04 · Investment

The money: programmes, orders and capital markets

There is no programme aimed specifically at infrared detectors. Money reaches the field indirectly, through the semiconductor mission, the electronics component scheme and iDEX challenges. On the private side, Tonbo Imaging's listing in October 2026 gave Indian thermal imaging its first public-market valuation, at about ₹4,000 crore at the top of the price band.

05c · Talent

Who builds it: where the people come from, and who is training the next wave

India has strong optics, electronics and image-software talent, built in DRDO labs, BEL, the ordnance optics factories and private firms like Tonbo. What it lacks is engineers who have run a detector fab, because India has never had one at volume.

06 · History

Seeing in the dark: 1990s → 2030

India met its night-vision needs with imports and licences in the 1990s. Indian-designed sights arrived in the 2010s. The 2020s brought germanium export controls, iDEX detector challenges and the first listing of an Indian thermal company, but not yet an Indian detector.

08 · Roadmap

How far, realistically?

Our assessment of when each level of “indigenous” becomes realistic, given current projects, funding and global supply chains.

Level 1
Today

Indian sights, foreign detectors

  • Indian design, electronics and software
  • Imported detectors, germanium and displays
  • Indian makers win Indian orders

~29% Indian-owned value.

Level 2
2026–2030

Indian optics and a licensed detector line

  • Germanium-free and chalcogenide lenses
  • A licensed microbolometer line in India
  • Indian readout chips

~50% Indian-owned value. Needs a licence deal and guaranteed orders.

Level 3
2030–2035

Indian detector design and coolers

  • Indian-designed microbolometers
  • Indian Stirling cryocoolers
  • Indian thermal cores exported

~75% Indian-owned value.

Level 4
2035+

100% indigenous

  • Indian cooled detectors
  • Indian microdisplays and imaging chips
  • Indian germanium refining

Only the US, France, Israel and China come close.
