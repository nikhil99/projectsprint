Project Sprint · tracking India's deep-tech progress

How far is India from a 100% indigenous AI phone?

A smartphone is a stack of a dozen technologies — chip, modem, memory, display, camera, battery, board, body, factory, operating system, AI models and a brand. India is world-class at some, absent from others. This tracker scores each layer, names the companies and the money behind them, and lists exactly what still blocks a phone that is Indian all the way down.

Why official “value addition” looks higher

The government's 25–28% domestic value addition counts work done in India — assembly, testing, locally made chargers, enclosures and packaging — regardless of who owns the technology. Our ~18% counts only value from Indian-owned design and production. Both are correct; they answer different questions.

What the 2028 target actually requires

“Indian-designed, Indian-manufactured, Indian-branded” means owning the phone's system design (board, industrial design, software) and the brand, plus building it here. It does not require an Indian processor, modem, memory or OS — which is why it is achievable by 2028 while a fully indigenous phone is not.

05c · Talent

Who builds it: where the people come from, and who is training the next wave

Semicon 1.0 (December 2021) set off India's first organised push to train chip and electronics talent. Today's experienced workforce mostly comes from foreign companies' design centres in India, Indians returning from the global chip industry, and staff trained by foreign partners. The next wave is coming through colleges and new national programmes.

Leaders and their backgrounds

Design talent is deep, but works for othersTexas Instruments opened India's first multinational design centre in Bengaluru in 1985. Since then Intel, Qualcomm, Nvidia, MediaTek and others have built a large pool, yet few Indian chip products have come out of it.
Fab talent is being imported and borrowedTata has sent a few hundred staff to Taiwan in batches of 50–75 to train at PSMC, and hires veterans from Intel and GlobalFoundries. Fab leaders are mostly returnees.
Phone-brand know-how comes from Chinese brandsAi+'s founder Madhav Sheth previously ran Realme India. Indian brands are recruiting the managers who built Chinese brands' India businesses.

08 · Roadmap

How far, realistically?

Our assessment of when each level of “indigenous” becomes realistic, given current projects, funding and global supply chains.

Level 1
2027–28

Designed, made & branded in India

  • Indian-owned board design, industrial design, software
  • Assembled under MPMS; Indian brand sells it
  • Indian on-device AI (Sarvam Edge-class) for 22 languages
  • Foreign SoC, modem, memory, Android

~20–25% Indian-owned value. On track — the government's stated target.

Level 2
2029–31

Indian chips around the processor

  • Power-management, display-driver, audio, sensor-hub chips designed in India, made at Tata Dholera (28–110 nm)
  • Memory packaged at Micron Sanand / Tata Assam
  • Display & camera modules with Indian sub-components

~30–35% Indian-owned value. Plausible if Dholera ramps on time.

Level 3
2031–35

Indian-designed AI processor

  • Indian SoC with own NPU, on licensed Arm or RISC-V cores, fabbed at TSMC/Samsung
  • Indian 4G/5G modem (licensed patents)
  • Hardened AOSP fork with Indian app store & services

~50–60% Indian-owned value. Needs a ₹10,000+ cr, decade-long fabless champion.

Level 4
2035+ / maybe never

100% indigenous

  • Leading-edge (≤5 nm) fab in India
  • Indian DRAM/NAND, OLED panels, image sensors, battery cells
  • Independent OS ecosystem

No country does this alone — not the US, China or Korea. A goal of “Indian-controlled”, not “100% Indian”, is the realistic target.
